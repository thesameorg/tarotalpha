How the forecast is computed

Every number on the site comes from the formulas below. They are deterministic and open, and they are not financial advice: the cards are a seeded random-number generator in a costume.

The whole calculation at a glance

Step What goes in What comes out
1. Snapshot 168 closed hourly candles up to the anchor, straight from the exchange the history every formula reads: the scale and anything indicator-shaped
2. Seed of the day asset|anchor_ts|day|nonce the starting number of the generator
3. Cards of the day the seed's generator three distinct cards, each upright or reversed
4. Noise of the day a second generator, seeded noise|reader| plus the seed a stream of random numbers of this reader's own
5. Candles of the day the reader's formula: snapshot, earlier forecast days, cards, noise 24 candles, eight per card
6. Words the cards and the 24 candles the card effects and the day's digest, which the interface turns into prose
7. Check forecast candles against the real candles of the same hours accuracy and drift
8. Rating the drift of all five readers on the score's own readings places, ratings, stars

1. The seed and the reading's own entropy

A reading is fixed by four values: the instrument, the anchor, the day and the nonce. The anchor is the open time of the last closed hourly candle; the nonce is 64 random bits your browser draws for this reading, so two readings of the same instrument in the same hour are never the same cards.

  • Seed string: asset|anchor_ts|day|nonce, for example BTCUSDT|1789020000000|1|9f3c2b7e41a05d68.
  • Hash: xmur3 of the seed string feeds a mulberry32 generator. The candle noise uses a second generator seeded with noise|reader| plus the same string, so the chosen reader changes the candles and never the cards.
  • The nonce is stored with the reading, so a link replays the author's cards. A reading shared before nonces existed has none, and its seed is the three fields before it.

2. The deck and the draw

78 cards of the Rider–Waite–Smith deck. Majors are ids 0–21 in the classic order (0 the Fool … 16 the Tower … 21 the World); minors are 22 + suit × 14 + (rank − 1) with wands, cups, swords, pentacles and ranks ace (1) … ten (10), page (11), knight (12), queen (13), king (14).

A day draws three distinct cards: id = floor(r() × 78), a repeat is skipped without spending a second number, and an accepted card is reversed when r() < 0.28. The fan of backs on screen is theatre: whichever back you pull, the i-th pull reveals card i.

3. The scale, and what is not in the seed

The seed gives the cards and the noise. Nothing else: every indicator-shaped number is measured on the snapshot.

  • natr — ATR(14) of the snapshot divided by its last close: the plain mean of the true range over the last 14 candles, no Wilder smoothing, as a fraction of price. 0.008 means the hour moves 0.8 % on average. Measured once per reading.
  • A — the amplitude unit of a candle: natr × previous close. One ATR is the same fraction of price on every candle, so the price can never cross zero, however bad the cards.
  • All five readers share that unit: they differ by formula, not by scale. Everything else each one needs — a mean, a trend, a matching stretch of history — she measures herself, listed in section 5.

4. What every candle obeys

  • A card rules eight candles in a row: the first card hours 1–8, the second 9–16, the third 17–24.
  • A candle opens at the previous close. There are no gaps: crypto never closes, so the Tower and the Sun put their jump into the body of the block's first candle instead.
  • Wicks are a random addition from the noise. A candle narrower than 0.3 · A gets both wicks widened to it, which spends no number of the generator.
  • Day 2 continues day 1: the formula sees the snapshot and every forecast candle drawn before it. The same seed always gives the same candles, to the last bit.

5. The five readers

A reader is who computes the forecast. All five draw the same three cards — the reader is not part of the shuffle seed — and turn them into different candles. She is part of the noise seed instead, so one reading never gives two readers the same forecast, and she is stored with a shared reading, so a link replays the reader its author saw.

Reader What she measures on the history The close of a candle What it looks like
atr — Madame Vera ma, the mean of the last 24 closes; dir, the sign of the move over 24 candles, zero reads as up o + (jump + drift) · A + (r() − 0.5) · v · A + pull · (ma − o) an even breath around the trend, rare outliers
reversion — Sister Anemone mean, the mean of the last 20 closes, used as a target o + pull · (mean + shove · A − o) + (r() − 0.5) · v · A swings in a corridor, almost no trend
analogy — Elder Kofi the 8 stretches of the snapshot whose 12 returns are closest to the last 12; flow, the trend sign over 24 candles o × exp(sign · gain · move of the copy) + jump · A a plausible shape: the market repeating itself
garch — Mama Ife nothing beyond the scale o × (1 + shock) + (lean + jump) · A, the shock drawn from the variance omega + alpha · move² + beta · variance quiet zones and bursts in a row
fractal — The Weaver nothing beyond the scale o + (0.6 · gain · Σ Hurst weights × the last 12 shocks + lean + jump) · A one long smooth run, or a fine saw

The knobs a card turns:

  • atr: drift, the push per candle in A; v, the width of the noise, never below 0.6; jump; pull, the tug towards ma. Only the Wheel and Death rewrite dir, and it stays rewritten to the end of the day.
  • reversion: pull, the share of the way to the target per candle; v; shove, a one-candle overshoot past the target; shift, a move of the target itself per candle in A. The target does not follow the price on its own — if it did, this reader would slide into a trend and repeat atr.
  • analogy: a number of the noise picks which of the eight matches to take, the Fool takes the farthest one; sign copies the stretch as it is or mirrored; gain is the loudness. The copy is the eight returns that followed the match. Its mean is removed, or a rising week would tilt every forecast up, and it is stretched to a move of 0.7 NATR an hour (between ×0.5 and ×4), or a quiet week of history would draw a flat line of dots.
  • garch: alpha, how sharply the variance answers a move; beta, how long it remembers; lean, the push per candle in A. A calm move is 0.6 NATR an hour, and the variance is clamped between 0.1 and 16 calm ones.
  • fractal: H, the Hurst exponent — above 0.5 the moves agree and the price travels, below 0.5 they argue and it saws. The weights (k+1)^(H−0.5) − k^(H−0.5) are normalised, so H changes the character of the walk and never its width. gain is the loudness, lean the push. The memory of 12 shocks starts full, or the first candles of a reading would be quieter than the rest.

The wicks of the atr reader are h = max(o, c) + r() · v · A · 0.6 and l = min(o, c) − r() · v · A · 0.6. Its generator call order per candle: swords take one number for the sign of the push, then the close noise, then h, then l. The scale is calibrated so that the mean true range of a forecast candle is about one ATR.

7. The day summary

Under the three cards the interface writes one paragraph from the day's numbers:

  • netPct — the last close against the first open, in percent; highPct, lowPct — the extremes from the same open. The paragraph prints percentages only: the unit a reader measures in is a technical fact and lives on this page, not in eleven languages.
  • Direction: flat when the net move is under half an ATR, otherwise up or down. ATR is the engine's own yardstick here whatever the reader, so the wording of a quiet day means the same thing everywhere.
  • The ruling card: the strongest major on the table in the order Tower, Sun, Death, Wheel, Moon, Hanged Man, Fool, then any other major, then the highest-ranked minor; a tie goes to the earlier position.
  • A regime flip is flagged when the Wheel or Death is on the table.
  • The closing sentence is picked from the sum of card ids and orientations, so one reading always reads the same.

8. The prophecy check

When a link is opened after its anchor, the browser fetches the real candles that have closed since and compares each forecast candle with the real candle of the same hour. A candle without a real pair does not count.

  • Accuracy — the share of candles whose direction matches: (fc.c ≥ fc.o) === (real.c ≥ real.o), that is hits / compared. It is interim until all 24 × days candles have a real pair, and 50 % or more reads as "the prophecy came true". Over many readings it lands, honestly, near one half.
  • Drift — the mean gap between the closes, |fc.c − real.c|, divided by the ATR(14) of the snapshot in price. It ignores the sign, because the sign is what accuracy measures. The unit is the market's and the same for everybody, so five readers and any two instruments are comparable in it.
  • A word, not a number. The badge shows the percentage and one word about the reader: close to the market, near it, or wide of it. The judgement is drift / √compared, because two random walks drift apart with the square root of their length. The thresholds 0.35 and 0.9 are the first and third quartiles of that value on live candles (5 instruments × 3 anchors × 5 readers, horizons of 6, 24 and 48 candles), so praise and scorn each land on about a quarter of readings.

9. The reader rating

The stars on a reader's card are not an opinion. The score keeps its own track — one instrument, one reading every four hours, drawn by a cron and by nobody else — and once a reading's horizon has closed, all five forecasts are rebuilt from that one row and measured against the same real candles. Readings people draw are never scored: otherwise the rating would say which coin was popular this week.

  • A place, not a threshold. On one reading the five are sorted by drift: +1 to the first, −1 to the last, evenly in between, zero across the table. A fixed threshold would measure the week instead of the reader — on a calm one everybody would pass, on a stormy one everybody would fail.
  • The rating forgets. It is an exponential mean of places with a memory of twenty readings: a reading loses half its weight within seven, and 95 % of it after thirty.
  • Stars are a place on the scale. 3 + rating / spread, where the spread is the root mean square of the table's ratings; clamped to 1…5 in halves. Five similar mechanics all sit near the middle, so on an absolute scale everyone would score three forever; the full five goes to whoever pulls two spreads clear of the rest. Under ten scored readings there are no stars at all: a guessed star reads as a measured one.

10. What a link stores, and what it recomputes

Stored: the cards of every written day, the 168-candle snapshot the server took itself, the exchange it came from, the reader and the nonce. Those are the reading. Recomputed in your browser on every view, with the formulas of today: the forecast candles, the card effects, the day summary, the accuracy and the drift. The verdict behind the stars is computed once, by the cron, and kept.

So a link can differ from what its author saw, in exactly these ways:

  • A formula was edited: the candles and the accuracy are new, the cards and the snapshot are not.
  • The server snapshotted a different exchange than the author's browser did — Binance answers 403 to the Cloudflare edge — so the snapshots differ by hundredths of a percent. The cards are the same, the candles slightly different, and analogy may pick another matching stretch and draw a visibly different shape.
  • A tab open across a deploy that changed the shuffle: the first day is refused with a 409, because the cards it drew are not the ones the server draws. Later days are not checked and are appended by the server's engine.
  • Formulas were edited after a verdict was scored: the stars were counted with the old ones while the badge on a link is counted with the new, until the exponential memory washes the old verdicts out.
